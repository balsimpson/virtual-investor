# Financial Analysis for Indian Equities

## Analysis Order

Work from primary filings outward:

1. Exchange-filed financial results, annual report, and material announcements
2. Company investor presentation and earnings transcript
3. Credit-rating rationale and regulator or government data
4. Reputable reporting that names its sources
5. Aggregators and broker estimates for comparison only

Record the period, standalone or consolidated basis, units, publication time,
and source for every material figure. Never compare mismatched periods or
standalone results with consolidated consensus.

## Non-Financial Companies

Assess:

- revenue growth by price, volume, mix, currency, acquisition, and accounting
- gross, EBITDA, EBIT, and free-cash-flow margins across a full cycle
- return on capital and incremental return on new capital
- operating cash flow versus PAT and EBITDA
- maintenance versus growth capex
- net debt, interest coverage, repayment schedule, and covenant headroom
- receivable, inventory, payable, and cash-conversion days
- dilution, buybacks, dividends, acquisitions, and capital allocation
- segment economics rather than consolidated headline growth alone
- valuation versus the company's history, direct peers, and scenario earnings

Do not use universal “good” thresholds across sectors. Compare trends, cyclicality,
accounting definitions, and the economics of direct peers.

## Banks

Use a lender-specific framework:

- net interest margin and spread
- loan and deposit growth
- CASA and funding concentration
- gross and net NPA
- slippages, recoveries, write-offs, and credit cost
- provision coverage and restructured book
- CET1 and total capital adequacy
- return on assets and return on equity
- unsecured and sector concentration
- asset-liability maturity and liquidity coverage
- price to adjusted book value and sustainable RoA/RoE

Do not apply EBITDA, current ratio, or ordinary net-debt rules to banks.

## NBFCs and Housing Finance

Assess:

- AUM mix and growth
- borrowing mix, cost of funds, spreads, and ALM gaps
- stage 2 and stage 3 assets, credit cost, and provisions
- capital adequacy and leverage
- collection efficiency and vintage performance
- securitization or co-lending dependence
- liquidity buffers and refinancing needs
- price to book or earnings in the context of asset quality

## Sector-Specific Drivers

| Sector | Required operating drivers |
|---|---|
| IT services | constant-currency growth, deal wins, utilization, attrition, margin, vertical mix |
| Pharma | geography and product mix, R&D, USFDA status, launches, price erosion |
| Auto | volumes, realizations, commodity costs, mix, discounts, inventory, EV capex |
| FMCG | volume versus price growth, rural/urban mix, gross margin, distribution |
| Metals | commodity curve, cost position, utilization, capex, leverage through cycle |
| Infrastructure | executable order book, working capital, guarantees, project cash flow |
| Telecom | ARPU, subscribers, churn, spectrum liabilities, capex, free cash flow |
| Real estate | presales, collections, project cash flow, land payments, net debt |
| Energy | volumes, regulated versus market pricing, refining or marketing margins |

## Earnings Quality and Governance

Investigate:

- receivables or inventory growing materially faster than revenue
- operating cash flow persistently trailing reported profit
- recurring “one-off” adjustments or other income supporting PAT
- capitalized expenses, changes in depreciation, or accounting-policy shifts
- related-party transactions, loans, guarantees, and subsidiary complexity
- contingent liabilities and regulatory or tax disputes
- promoter pledging, dilution, preferential allotments, and insider activity
- auditor qualifications, resignations, delayed results, or internal-control issues
- guidance changes that are not reconciled with earlier statements

Treat a red flag as a research question, not automatic proof of misconduct.

## Expectation and Scenario Analysis

State:

- the sourced market expectation and timestamp
- the operational variable where the thesis differs
- bull, base, and bear assumptions
- the earnings or cash-flow impact of each assumption
- the valuation multiple appropriate to each scenario
- target and downside values consistent with those scenarios
- what new evidence would change the probabilities

Do not infer consensus solely from price action, social-media sentiment, or one
broker note. If reliable consensus is unavailable, say so and frame the thesis
as a hypothesis about embedded expectations.

## Financial Summary Required in a Thesis

Write a concise, decision-relevant summary covering:

- growth source and durability
- margin and cash-conversion quality
- balance-sheet and refinancing risk
- governance or accounting concerns
- sector-specific operating KPI
- valuation assumption
- the figure most likely to falsify the thesis

Store detailed research in the research library and evidence ledger. Keep the
thesis summary short enough to inspect before every trade.
